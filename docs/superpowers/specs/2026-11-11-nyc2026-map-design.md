# NYC 2026 Map — Design Spec
**Date:** 2026-11-11
**Status:** Built and live (nyc2026.kevintraywick.com, nyc2026.kevintraywick.com/wendy)

---

## Overview

A full-screen map of New York for the December 4–7 2026 trip (Kevin, Amy, Lisa), and a second map on the same code for Wendy's Williamsburg weekend (November 21–22). Each place worth knowing about is a mark on the map; pointing at it opens an information pane with a photo, a short write-up, and the walk from the hotel.

It replaced a page with tile columns either side of the map, a route builder, and a day timeline.

## Who it is for

Amy and Lisa are not computer people, and neither is the brief for Wendy. Every decision is judged on one question: **would they get it without being told?** That rules out modes, toggles with hidden state, and anything that moves or changes under the pointer.

## Decisions, and why

| Decision | Why |
|---|---|
| The map fills the screen; no side columns, no timeline, no route builder | One thing to look at. The route builder was never going to be used and would confuse. |
| Every place is a mark on the map, not a tile in a list | Where a place is matters more than its name when you are deciding what to walk to. |
| The information pane is fixed at the far right | Three designs were compared: pane beside the dot, map plus list, colour map with a docked pane. A pane beside the dot covered its neighbours and was jarring. At the far right the map stays put and readable. |
| The pane never closes by itself | It is a long way from the dot, and there has to be time to travel over and read it. It closes with ✕, Esc, or a click on the map. |
| A short pause before a hover counts | The pointer crosses other dots on its way to the pane; the words must not change under it. |
| Walking time and the route from the hotel are kept | The one piece of the old page that was actually useful. |
| Grey map, coloured marks | The marks are the only colour, so they are the only thing the eye goes to. |
| No "Show the whole map" button | Removed on request; the zoom buttons are enough. |
| On a phone the pane is a sheet from the bottom | Same content, same behaviour, different place. |

## What the marks mean

| Mark | Trip map | Wendy's map |
|---|---|---|
| Yellow dot | A place to see | Something to do |
| Orange dot | A restaurant | A place to eat |
| Orange star | On North America's 50 Best Restaurants 2026 (13 of them) | One of three picks for Saturday dinner |
| Black circle, white star | Our hotel | Your hotel |
| Black circle, ticket | Our show | Your show |

The legend at the bottom left says the same thing in the same words, and the hotel's legend icon matches its map pin exactly.

A starred place also shows a line in its pane saying why it is starred: its rank on the list (Atomix carries both its world and North America rank), or "A good one for Saturday night".

## The header

The trip's days across the top, each with its weather and two or three plain lines (flights, check-in, the show). The trip map has four days and the invoice links; Wendy's has two days and no invoices.

Weather says where its numbers come from: the live forecast inside 16 days, the same dates last year before that, or what the month is usually like.

## The invoices (trip map only)

Joke "Guido & Sons" PDF invoices for the flights, seats and Broadway tickets Kevin fronted. A person marked paid gets a receipt in place of an invoice: a crooked red PAID stamp, a $0.00 balance, and Luigi's threat swapped for a cannoli. Amy and Lisa are both paid, so Kevin's settlement is stamped ALL SQUARE. A ✓ sits by a paid name in the header.

## Wendy's map

- Starts on Williamsburg only: her hotel (Pod Brooklyn, 247 Metropolitan Ave), her show (Rising Appalachia at National Sawdust, Sunday 7:30), eleven things to do and twelve places to eat, all a walk away.
- Her brief for dinner was "not too fancy, but not a food truck", so the places to eat are neighbourhood restaurants, with three starred for Saturday night.
- One link under the zoom buttons, **Include Manhattan**, adds every place from the trip map and zooms out to hold them all. It then reads **Back to Brooklyn**.
- Wording is hers throughout: "your hotel", "your show".

## Known gaps

- With Manhattan included on Wendy's map, those write-ups are still the trip's ("close to our hotel", "in December").
- Eight of Wendy's places and two of the trip's restaurants (Sushi Sho, Saga) have no photo.
- Restaurant photos are the restaurants' and the 50 Best list's own press pictures, not free-licence images.
- Opening hours for Wendy's weekend were not checked place by place.
- Michelin's 2026 New York stars had not been announced when the stars were added; the ★ marks in the restaurant subtitles are last year's.
